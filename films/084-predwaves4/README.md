# 084 · AURIC PREDICTIVE WAVES IV · FIXED ERROR, FINITE MEMORY AND THE RETURNED RECORD · AURIC 相容预测波 第四部 · 固定误差、有限记忆与回返记录

This is part four of five in the AURIC PREDICTIVE WAVES series and runs about 7½ minutes (7:27). It covers
`AURIC_COMPATIBLE_PREDICTIVE_WAVES_AND_RELATIONAL_SPECTRA_VI.md`, §72–§121, at trureturing subject commit
`24d79207e4`.

Earlier parts built the five-dimensional instrument, the compatible set K_d of qutrit states and the complete
labelled records of their time words. Volume VI first lets a finite classical imitator make a small fixed error and
counts the memory it then needs. It then fixes one pure preparation and one mixing control N, and asks which classical
models, effects, stopping rules, record anchors and record returns the same source supports or excludes.

## The film, scene by scene

- **Two worlds (§72–§74).**
  - With η = d(1 − d)/100, ρ± = ρ* ± tΔ, Δ = i(ξe† − eξ†) and t = ¼ min{λ_min(ρ*), λ_min(B_d(ρ*))}, both worlds are
    strictly positive members of K_d with five-dimensional CPTP lifts from the source contract, and t/4 > d(1 − d)/3200
    (Theorem 73.1).
  - Their records differ by P₊,ₙ(h, 1) − P₋,ₙ(h, 1) = 2t·2⁻ⁿ·b(h), with b(h) = Im(u†B_h u), B₀ = R diag(1, z),
    B₁ = B₀Z, z = e^(−2iθ). The group generated by B₀, B₁ is infinite and irreducible, and the Cesàro mean of
    a_n = 2⁻ⁿ Σ b(h)² over the actual fair words is exactly 1/4, without a mixing premise (Theorem 74.1).
- **Suffix filter (§75–§77).**
  - For any finite comparator with nonnegative branches, Λ = T₀ ⊗ B₀ + T₁ ⊗ B₁ has no fixed vector on any recurrent
    class that writes every word (Jensen equality and a finite coset cover, Lemma 75.1, Theorem 75.2).
  - A legal four-state cycle that writes only label 0 keeps an unfiltered average correlation of exactly 3/8 at
    z = (−3 + 4i)/5 (Proposition 76.1).
  - Scoring only records whose last m labels contain every word of length L sends every comparator's signed
    correlation to zero on average, while the target keeps A = 4t·v with average v ≥ 1/4 − δ_L,m and
    δ_L,m = min{1, 2^L(1 − 2^(−L))^⌊m/L⌋} → 0 (Theorem 77.1).
- **Error floor and memory (§78–§79).**
  - At every angle with sin 2θ ≠ 0, every finite comparator with any two preparations has depth-averaged total
    variation from the two complete record laws with lower limit at least t/4 > d(1 − d)/3200 (Theorem 78.1).
  - For a fixed error below d(1 − d)/3200, the number of classical states needed to match every endpoint up to
    horizon H tends to infinity, for the pair and for all of K_d. Each finite horizon is still matched exactly, for
    example by a tree of |N_H|·2^|N_H| states (Theorem 78.3). The volume states the scope of this result in §79.
- **Permutations (§80–§83).**
  - A reversible comparator updates by three permutations, with branch weights P_j/2 and P_N. Every finite
    prefix-closed word set D has an exact one with |D| states (Theorem 81.1), for example |D₁₂| = 797161 and
    |D₁₂,₂| = 167935 (Corollary 81.2). These are existence bounds, one model per depth.
  - No single finite one matches every word, because the readouts q_w are dense in [0, 1] (Theorem 82.1).
  - For any finite D, Malcev's residual finiteness gives a finite model that also keeps every group identity
    (Theorem 83.2).
- **Prediction quotient and hidden closure (§84–§90).**
  - If a model matches the four tables and every reachable difference missed by its nine rows is invisible to all
    legal futures, its prediction quotient is exactly nine-dimensional and every word matches (Theorem 84.2).
  - Countable and continuous reversible classical models satisfy this. With the labels kept,
    H(X_W | W) = H(μ) and H(X_W, W) = H(μ) + k log 2 (Theorem 85.1, Proposition 85.2).
  - Requiring the stronger hidden measure equalities fails on every measurable space. The induced event effects must
    commute with the sharp terminal E, which leaves five real dimensions to separate nine (Theorem 87.3).
  - In both infinite models one explicit residual is nonzero although every legal future reads zero
    (Proposition 88.1). A time-order lottery has equal reduced blocks but full archives at trace distance 2λm
    (Proposition 89.3).
- **One twelfth (§91–§94).**
  - For every installed mixing N there is a rank-one P_N with 1/8 ≤ |f†Nᵐv_N|² ≤ 7/8 for all m. Its witness
    Z_N = 2P_N − I scores at most 3/4 on every bounded complete-record effect of a fixed schedule. So every such effect
    stays at least 1/12 from P_N (Theorem 92.1). Nine weighted prefix rows show a nonzero deviation (Corollary 93.2).
  - The closed cone of all record effects is still all of PSD₃ (Corollary 94.1).
- **Stopping (§95–§100).**
  - Allowing a stop decided only by past labels, one fixed sweep T(θ, N, ε), the same for every rank-one target P and
    independent of d, has stopping tables with ‖F_π − P‖ < ε and uniform total variation below ε, with every timeout
    and mismatch kept in the record (Theorem 96.1).
  - There is no call budget uniform over mixing controls near the identity:
    ‖F − P_ξ‖ ≥ 2/5 − 2B‖N − I‖ for trees with at most B calls of N (Theorem 97.1). Exact finite acquisition needs
    a target among countably many word projections (Proposition 97.2).
  - Relabelling one binary terminal into three outcomes gives (1/3) Σ Tr(P_i M_i) ≤ 2/3, so
    max_i ‖M_i − P_i‖ ≥ 1/3 for every orthonormal rank-one basis (Proposition 98.1). The pre-terminal labels alone do
    not identify the model (Proposition 99.2).
- **Record anchor (§101–§102).**
  - With access to the emitted record, every complete repeatable lift has the form L_γ with one γ in the closed unit
    disk. A fixed Hadamard readout gives p₊(γ) = (1 + ω_d Re γ)/2 with ω_d = Tr(Wρ₀) = (2 − d)/4. So the anchor
    (6 − d)/8 holds exactly when γ = 1, which fixes every cross block, history and untouched reference
    (Theorems 101.3 and 102.1).
  - γ = 0 gives 1/2 on this readout but the same native records. An irreversible label archive removes the cross
    blocks (Theorem 102.2).
- **Entanglement (§103–§105, §111).**
  - With Θ_N = Λ² ∘ Ad_N ∘ Λ², r_N < 1 exactly when [N, P_ξ] ≠ 0, and ‖ρₙ − I/3‖ ≤ √(2/3) r_Nⁿ. With coherent
    records the system and its own record carriers approach maximal entanglement, entropy log 3 (Theorem 103.2).
  - One interaction alone has Schmidt weights (6 − d)/8 and (2 + d)/8. Without N the marginal tends to
    (d/2)P_ξ + ((1 − d/2)/2)(I − P_ξ), and there is no uniform rate as N → I (Theorem 103.3).
  - δ_N/8 ≤ 1 − r_N² ≤ (3/2)δ_N with δ_N = ‖NP_ξ − P_ξN‖₂² (Theorem 111.2).
  - Dephased records give the same marginals and records but a separable system–archive state (Theorem 105.1).
- **One return (§106–§110, §113).**
  - Send the emitted record back once through the same interaction, completed to a fixed ten-dimensional unitary.
    The terminal then sees the completion only through one vector h in the unit ball:
    J†F₁J restricted to R ⊗ R_env is [[D, th†], [ht†, bhh†]] with b = (3 + 2√2)/12 (Theorem 106.3).
  - Every faithful completion leaks an input subspace of dimension at least 4 + 3 − 5 = 2 entirely (Theorem 113.1).
  - No completion hides retention. Some prefix and some history of length at most 17 separate any two γ
    (Theorems 107.2, 108.1).
  - One explicit completion at θ = π/4 gives terminal masses (6 − 5d)/32 and (18 + d)/128. That is 1/12 against 7/48
    at d = 2/3, a terminal distance of 1/16, and 1/7 for both at d = 2/7, where the returned states still differ
    (Theorem 109.1).
- **Identification (§112, §114–§121).**
  - Erasing labels merges the orthogonal pair (e ± f)/√2, so every recovery misses one of them by at least 1/2
    (Theorem 112.1).
  - For two devices with unknown, different completions, the full unconditioned return laws always determine γ. At zero
    retention they confuse exactly the reflected pairs (x + iy)t and (−x + iy)t (Theorem 114.3, Corollary 115.1).
  - Finitely many rows give a retention modulus with m = 5/8 − b = (9 − 4√2)/24 > 0 (Theorem 116.4,
    Corollary 117.1). They also give a two-sided comparison on the whole domain (Theorem 118.3) and an inverse
    constant for h of exact order 1/ρ on |γ| ≥ ρ (Theorem 119.2).
  - At zero-retention singleton anchors the error modulus is of order ε off the complex line of t and of order √ε at
    imaginary multiples of t (Theorem 120.3). On the restricted family Re⟨e, h₀⟩ = 0 it is uniformly of order
    ε/(η + √ε) (Theorem 121.2), and no such bound holds over all singleton anchors (Proposition 121.3).
